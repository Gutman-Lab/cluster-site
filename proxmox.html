Proxmox, briefly

Proxmox Virtual Environment (PVE) is a Debian-based virtualization platform. A PVE cluster is a group of physical hosts that share configuration, authentication, and a quorum service; each host can run QEMU virtual machines and LXC containers, and VMs can migrate between hosts.

Cluster and quorum

Nodes join a cluster through Corosync. A majority of votes must be present for the cluster to make changes. pvecm status shows membership and quorum.

Storage, local or shared

PVE can use local disks (LVM, ZFS, directories) or shared storage such as NFS, iSCSI, or Ceph RBD. Shared storage is what makes live migration and HA practical.

Ceph inside Proxmox

PVE can deploy and manage Ceph directly. Each host contributes OSDs; pools are exposed to PVE as storage. Replication and placement are handled by Ceph, not by RAID.

Backups and HA

Proxmox Backup Server provides incremental, deduplicated backups. HA moves or restarts guests when a node fails, but only for resources explicitly placed under HA management.

Why this matters for the tour

The cluster's flexibility comes from two layers: Proxmox decides where a VM runs, and Ceph decides where its blocks live. A VM's disk can be on a pool backed by HDDs, SATA SSDs, NVMe, or Optane-accelerated SSDs without changing anything inside the guest.

pmoxprime at a glance

pmoxprime is the production-adjacent cluster: fifteen Proxmox hosts, a seven-monitor Ceph quorum, and roughly half a petabyte of raw Ceph capacity. It is not a uniform deployment. Nodes differ in CPU generation, RAM population, drive count, and role.

RAM and network

Total RAM is roughly 5.2 TiB of DDR4 ECC, a mix of 16 GB and 32 GB modules at 2133–2666 MT/s. Population is uneven by design: the all-flash compute nodes carry 503 GiB each, while the HDD hosts carry 188–377 GiB. Several nodes have empty DIMM slots, so memory can be expanded without replacing what is already there.

Ceph uses two networks. Replication (OSD-to-OSD) runs on a dedicated 25 GbE fabric — 172.16.25.0/24, Mellanox ConnectX-4 Lx on the upgraded nodes, MTU 9000. Client traffic (RADOS, CephFS, RBD) runs on the public network — 10.10.10.0/24 over a 10 GbE bridge at MTU 1500, shared with VM and management traffic. The two are separated on purpose, but it means a single-client benchmark is capped by 10 GbE no matter how fast the OSDs are. Moving the public network onto the 25 GbE fabric is the next networking change.

Ceph: four tiers, one cluster

The architectural decision that makes heterogeneity work is the CRUSH device-class hierarchy. Each drive class gets its own rule, and each workload gets a pool that maps to the rule it needs. Ceph still treats the cluster as one pool of storage, but placement is explicit instead of accidental.

BlueFS DB/WAL offload

Not every OSD keeps its data and metadata on the same device. HDD OSDs put their write-ahead log on NVMe; Optane-backed OSDs pair a 3.49 TB SATA SSD with a dedicated 64 GB NVMe partition for the BlueFS DB, with WAL co-located. This is the single biggest reason the HDD tier is competitive on small writes.

Bulk capacity is not the sum of the drives. The EC pools are 4+2 with a host failure domain, so usable capacity is raw HDD space divided by 1.5, while the free-space figure ceph df reports is set by the fullest OSD. Current status (22 November 2026)

109/109 OSDs up and in, 1,937 placement groups, 7/7 monitors quorate, and zero backfill or recovery during the 22 November benchmark campaign. Capacity: 542 TiB raw at roughly 38% used. The four device classes remain: HDD ~39% used, SSD ~35%, NVMe ~61%, Optane ~16%. Bulk rebalance is complete — there is no active backfill, only deep-scrubbing. The remaining Ceph warnings are an auth-key migration item, not a data-path problem.

What runs on it

Alongside Ceph, four TrueNAS SCALE hosts provide NFS-backed bulk storage and the whole-slide imaging archive: brainhost/slidevault (36 HDDs, 458 TiB raw), icarus/daghome (all-SSD home and compute scratch), scratchy/samsungssd (all-SSD scratch), and gauss/tank (HDD bulk). Two QNAP units hold archival WSI data and restic targets. The benchmark story for those hosts is on the benchmarks page.

A separate cluster, kubertpmox, handles disaster recovery and staging so that backups are not trapped inside the system they protect.
